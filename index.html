What if one person helped invent the way we look at data?

Every time you use a box plot, explore a data set, or hear the word “bit,” you are connected to John Tukey.

John Wilder Tukey was an American mathematician and statistician who changed statistics from simply proving ideas into a way of exploring, visualizing, and understanding real data. His work helped shape modern statistics, computer science, data science, election polling, engineering, and education research.

Life History

John Tukey’s life shows how curiosity, education, and problem-solving can influence many fields at once.

1915

John Wilder Tukey was born on June 16, 1915, in New Bedford, Massachusetts.

1936–1937

He earned his bachelor’s and master’s degrees in chemistry from Brown University.

1939

Tukey earned his Ph.D. in mathematics from Princeton University. His early academic work was in topology, a branch of mathematics.

World War II

During the war, Tukey worked on applied research problems. This helped move his career toward practical data analysis and statistics.

After World War II

He split his career between Princeton University and AT&T Bell Laboratories, two major centers of research and innovation.

1947

Tukey is credited with helping popularize the word “bit,” meaning a binary digit, which later became central to computer science.

1965

He became the founding chairman of Princeton University’s statistics department.

1973

Tukey received the National Medal of Science, one of the highest scientific honors in the United States.

1977

He published Exploratory Data Analysis, a book that changed how people study and visualize data.

2000

John Tukey died on July 26, 2000, in New Brunswick, New Jersey.

Mathematical Discoveries

Tukey’s discoveries were important because they made statistics more visual, practical, and useful for real-world data.

Exploratory Data Analysis

Exploratory Data Analysis, or EDA, is the process of looking at data first to find patterns, trends, unusual values, and questions before making formal conclusions.

Box-and-Whisker Plot

Tukey helped popularize the box plot, a graph that summarizes the median, quartiles, spread, and possible outliers in a data set.

Fast Fourier Transform

With James Cooley, Tukey helped develop the Cooley-Tukey Fast Fourier Transform algorithm, which allows computers to analyze signals much faster.

Tukey’s Range Test

Tukey’s range test is used after an ANOVA test to compare different group means and decide which groups are significantly different.

Stem-and-Leaf Displays

Tukey promoted simple visual displays, such as stem-and-leaf plots, that allow people to see the shape of data while keeping the original numbers visible.

Words in Technology

Tukey is connected to the terms “bit” and “software,” words that became essential in computer science and digital technology.

Contributions to Society

John Tukey’s work benefited humankind because it helped people make better decisions using data.

Better Data Visualization

Box plots and other visual tools help students, scientists, doctors, businesses, and governments understand large amounts of information quickly.

Modern Data Science

Exploratory Data Analysis encouraged people to investigate data visually before jumping to conclusions. This is a major part of modern data science.

Technology and Engineering

The Fast Fourier Transform is used in signal processing, image compression, medical imaging, audio technology, astronomy, and many computer applications.

Election Polling

Tukey helped design and analyze television network election polls, showing how statistics could explain public opinion and election results.

Education Research

He contributed to national education assessment work, helping researchers use data to understand student learning and school performance.

Computer Language

By helping introduce words like “bit” and “software,” Tukey influenced the language used in computer science and technology today.
